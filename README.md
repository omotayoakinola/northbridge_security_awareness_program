# Northbridge Logistics: 12 Month Security Awareness Program

 A practical security awareness program for a fictional logistics company with 800 staff across head office, warehouses and remote sales.

## What is in this repository

**GRC206_Security_Awareness_Program_2025_GCR_12813.pdf** is the full submission: a 6 page report covering assess, plan, deliver, measure and improve, followed by a one page 12 month program calendar.

## The scenario

Northbridge has 800 staff across head office, warehouses and remote sales. It handles regulated customer data, has faced recent invoice fraud attempts, and two staff reused passwords after a breach. Leadership wants fewer incidents within 12 months, with a security team too small to run a heavy program.

## Approach 

Every part of the program traces back to a real risk, so nothing is training for its own sake.

| Risk | Who is exposed | Target behaviour (Proposed target) | How it is measured |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Invoice fraud | Finance and procurement | 90% of payment changes verified by call back and a second approver by month 6 | Monthly finance sample audit |
| Password reuse | All staff | 95% multi factor coverage and 75% fewer reused or breached passwords by month 6 | Identity system reports |
| Customer data mishandling | Head office, warehouse, remote sales | 30% fewer human error incidents and 90% reported within 24 hours by month 12 | Incident log |

## The design decisions 
1. **Measure behaviour, not completion.** Six metrics cover reach, behaviour and outcomes, each with a calculation, data source, owner and baseline comparison. Completion proves reach. Verified payments and faster reporting prove risk is falling.
2. **Design for the warehouse, not just the office.** Warehouse staff touch customer data daily but rarely use email, so they get 10 minute supervisor led toolbox talks and QR code reporting instead of online modules and phishing clicks.
3. **Make reporting safe.** A no blame route, group level simulation results and recognition for good catches, so people report mistakes early instead of hiding them. A rising report rate is treated as progress, and the report explains why incident counts may look worse before they improve.
4. **Fit the team that has to run it.** Ownership sits with the people who control the evidence (Finance Controller, IT Security Lead, Data Protection Contact), and the calendar is paced for a security team of about three.
5. **Be honest about limits.** No baseline exists, so every target is labelled a planning choice, and leadership reports state limitations and the decisions needed from them.

## What this demonstrates

1. Translating scenario facts into prioritised human risks and measurable objectives
2. Role based training design for different audiences, including frontline staff
3. Metrics and leadership reporting that connect security activity to business risk
4. Governance thinking: sponsorship, ownership, quarterly reviews and a continuous improvement loop
5. Alignment with recognised practice, including ISO/IEC 27001:2022 control 6.3 and NIST SP 800-50
